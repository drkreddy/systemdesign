Every city caches separately

There isn't a cache. There are hundreds, one per city, and they don't share with each other.

This surprises almost everyone, and it quietly explains the hardest problem in the subject. To test it, the lab warmed a file from India — confirmed HIT — and then requested the same file from ten cities worldwide.

Nine of them missed.

Running the same ten cities again, once every edge held a copy:

RunResultMedian totalMedian TTFBReached Oregon
First visit per city1 HIT, 9 MISS429ms294ms9
Once every city is warm10 HIT99ms12ms0

TTFB fell from 294ms to 12ms, and the second run added zero origin requests. Ten more requests worldwide and the server in Oregon never heard about any of them.

Anycast doesn't always pick the nearest city

One assumption worth dropping. The measurements kept showing routing that geography wouldn't predict:

anycast
One address announced from hundreds of locations at once; your packets reach whichever the network considers closest. "Closest" is decided by BGP — which follows peering agreements and commercial relationships, not kilometres.

Those mis-routed probes were also the slowest. A bad colo assignment adds a leg to every journey, and it isn't something you control.

Where this is going

If one object can cost nine origin fetches simply by being popular in nine cities, consider what happens when you clear your cache. Every city loses its copy at the same moment, and every city asks your server at the same moment. That's post 6.
