How a CDN actually works

Six short posts. Every claim came from measuring a real setup — an instrumented server in Oregon behind Cloudflare — and every experiment can be re-run from your browser, right now, against that same infrastructure.

Most CDN explanations stop at "it keeps copies closer to users". That's true and it's not enough to work with. It doesn't tell you why turning a CDN on often caches nothing, why a cache can serve the wrong visitor's content while reporting perfect health, or why clearing your cache can make your site four times slower worldwide.

Each post below has a Run it live button. The recorded numbers appear instantly; pressing the button fires real requests from your browser and shows you what actually happens today.

How the live experiments work

Requests go to play.drkreddy.com, a deliberately small public surface in front of the real lab. Every parameter is capped, only a fixed list of experiments exists, and your requests are namespaced to you — so two readers running the same experiment see their own independent results rather than confusing each other.

If the live path is unavailable, busy, or rate limited, the widgets fall back to recorded data and say so. You should never see a broken box on a page whose job is explaining something.

A note on honesty

Several of these posts describe things I got wrong while building this, including a cache that reported perfect health while serving permanently frozen content. Those are kept in rather than tidied away, because the debugging is more useful than the conclusion.
